# Asa (آسا) — Full Marketing Plan

## Marketing objective

Acquire the first 20–30 paid, permissioned orders from a narrow Tehran service zone and learn which specific family task produces repeat demand. The campaign should sell **reliable help and evidence of completion**, not a broad promise to provide every form of eldercare.

## Positioning

> **Asa helps you take care of family in Tehran when you cannot be there in person.**

Persian direction:

> **آسا؛ کمک مطمئن برای مراقبت از خانواده، وقتی خودتان نمی‌توانید کنارشان باشید.**

Keep the original brand territory—peace of mind and family well-being—but replace any implication of guaranteed health or safety with concrete service proof: named worker, defined scope, receipts, arrival/completion messages, and authorized family updates.

## Personas and messages

### Diaspora adult child

**Job:** get a specific task done for a parent in Tehran without asking a relative to manage it.  
**Message:** “Choose the task, set the budget and timing, and receive a clear update when it is completed.”  
**Objections:** Is this legal? Can I pay? Who enters the home? What if the recipient refuses?  
**Proof:** provider approval, worker identification, consent, itemized receipt, clear exclusions, support response time.

### Local cross-city adult child

**Job:** coordinate help from another Iranian city.  
**Message:** “A defined local service, with one point of coordination.”  
**Objections:** Can you reach the neighborhood? What happens if plans change?  
**Proof:** service map, quoted travel fee, cancellation policy, local references.

### Recipient / older adult

**Job:** receive practical help without losing dignity or control.  
**Message:** “You choose what help is wanted; Asa supports the practical details.”  
**Objections:** stranger, privacy, being treated as helpless.  
**Proof:** recipient consent, worker introduction, ability to decline, respectful scripts.

## Message hierarchy

1. **Outcome:** a family task is completed locally.
2. **Trust:** the scope, price, worker, and update are clear.
3. **Dignity:** the recipient remains informed and in control.
4. **Convenience:** the payer can coordinate from another city or country.
5. **Continuity:** repeat tasks can be scheduled after the pilot proves reliable.

## Service copy

### Asa Shopping

**English headline:** Custom grocery help for family in Tehran.  
**Persian headline:** خرید و رساندن اقلام موردنیاز خانواده در تهران، با هماهنگی شفاف.  
**Body:** Send a list, budget, timing, and substitution preferences. Asa purchases locally, checks the order, delivers within the agreed area, and sends an itemized update. Product cost is separate from Asa’s service and delivery fees.

### Asa Medical Companion

**English headline:** Practical appointment support for a parent in Tehran.  
**Persian headline:** همراهی و هماهنگی عملی برای مراجعه پزشکی، بدون دخالت در امور درمانی.  
**Body:** Asa can coordinate practical details, accompany the recipient for a defined time, help with registration and waiting-room logistics, and send an authorized factual update. Asa is not a healthcare provider and does not diagnose, treat, administer medication, or give medical advice.

## Channel priorities

| Channel | First segment | Test | Keep/stop rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referrals | Diaspora families | Ask 10 trusted contacts for introductions; track paid orders, not clicks | Keep if 2+ paid orders or 5 qualified referrals per month |
| Search content | Diaspora adult children | Landing pages for “send groceries to parents in Tehran” and “someone accompany parent doctor Tehran” | Keep if qualified inquiry cost and conversion are acceptable after 4 weeks |
| Persian diaspora groups | Diaspora | Permission-based posts and partner introductions | Stop if group rules or payment/compliance conflict |
| Community ambassadors | High-trust communities | One small creator/ambassador per market with a tracked code | Keep only with documented paid conversion and transparent sponsorship |
| Local partnerships | Tehran recipients | Clinics/labs/suppliers that can ethically refer a non-clinical service | Require written scope and no improper referral incentives |
| Retention | First customers | Completion report, reorder reminder, prepaid bundle offer | Keep if repeat or referral rate improves without complaints |

Do not launch paid ads in a country until the payment gate for that market is cleared. Do not use a U.S. audience campaign for this model under current evidence.

## Funnel

1. Search, referral, or community introduction.
2. Service-specific page with zone checker and payment-status notice.
3. Short request form: recipient location, task, timing, consent, contact method.
4. Human confirmation and quote.
5. Payment through the approved route only.
6. Confirmation, service updates, completion evidence.
7. Follow-up: satisfaction, referral request, repeat-task reminder.

## Sample search campaigns

**Campaign A — Shopping**

- Headline: Grocery Help for Family in Tehran
- Headline: Send a Shopping List to Tehran
- Description: Local purchase, delivery, substitutions, and an itemized update. Defined service area. Request a quote.

**Campaign B — Appointment**

- Headline: Appointment Companion in Tehran
- Headline: Practical Support for a Parent’s Visit
- Description: Non-clinical coordination and accompaniment for a defined visit window. Consent and scope explained before booking.

Use only after payment approval and local operating readiness. Test exact-match/high-intent terms before broad display campaigns.

## SEO strategy

Create a small cluster, not hundreds of templated pages:

- /en/tehran-grocery-help-for-parents
- /fa/tehran-grocery-help
- /en/medical-appointment-companion-tehran
- /fa/hamrahi-pزشکی-tehran
- /en/how-asa-family-updates-work
- /en/faq-paying-for-family-support-in-iran

Content topics: how to send a shopping list, what a non-clinical companion does, how consent works, what happens when an item is unavailable, and how Asa separates product cost from service fees. Avoid medical advice and unsupported claims.

## Referral strategy

After a completed service, ask:

> “If someone in your family is trying to help a parent from a distance, would you feel comfortable introducing them to Asa? We can send them the same clear service scope and pricing.”

Use a modest service credit only if lawful and transparent. Never pay an undisclosed fee to a clinician or use a referral arrangement that could compromise professional judgment.

## Budget framework

Use test budgets, not annual commitments:

- Research/interviews/incentives: $300–$800.
- Website/forms/translation/basic creative: $500–$1,500.
- Paid search experiments after clearance: $500–$1,500.
- Community/ambassador tests: $300–$1,000.
- Referral credits: variable, capped per month.

Stop paid acquisition if the channel produces inquiries but no paid orders, or if contribution margin after acquisition cost is negative for two consecutive review periods.

## Metrics

Track qualified inquiries, quote-to-paid conversion, service revenue, contribution margin/order, CAC, on-time completion, complaint/refund rate, report completion, repeat purchase, referral rate, and cash burn. Use a channel-level source code and record payer country separately from recipient city.

## Brand voice

Warm but precise. Respectful, bilingual where useful, and never sentimental in a way that pressures adult children. Use “support,” “coordinate,” “accompany,” “confirm,” and “update.” Avoid “guaranteed safety,” “professional medical care,” “we are your parent’s family,” and claims that cannot be documented.
