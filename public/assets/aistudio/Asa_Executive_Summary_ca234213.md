# Asa (آسا) — Executive Summary

**Decision recommendation: REDESIGN / PROCEED CONDITIONALLY.** Asa has a credible customer problem and a potentially attractive asset-light service model, but the proposed international payment architecture is a **launch blocker today**. Current official provider evidence does not verify an approved end-to-end route for a foreign customer paying for grocery shopping or non-clinical appointment accompaniment fulfilled for a recipient in Iran. Stripe, PayPal, Wise, Coinbase Commerce/Business, and BitPay have published restrictions that conflict with the disclosed flow; Adyen is not verified without case-specific written approval. The U.S. customer market should remain excluded unless specialist sanctions review and written approvals clear the model.

## Payment-feasibility decision — read this first

Germany, Canada, the UK, Australia, and Turkey are **not shown by the reviewed sources to impose a blanket prohibition** on the narrowly defined service. However, each market remains conditional on the exact merchant entity, ownership and control, Iranian beneficiary and contractor screening, service scope, payment flow, settlement bank, tax classification, consumer disclosures, and local Iranian operating permissions. Iran’s FATF high-risk status and country-specific sanctions/AML rules make payment refusal, enhanced due diligence, holds, and account closure plausible even where the underlying service is not categorically prohibited.

The first launch gate is therefore not “which currency can be displayed?” It is: **will a named, regulated provider and settlement bank knowingly accept the fully disclosed flow, including Iran fulfillment and all onward payments?** Currency support, crypto support, a non-Iranian bank account, or an overseas payer is not evidence of approval.

Before any paid international order, Asa needs: an entity/nexus memo; a complete funds-flow map; sanctions screening for every party and owner/controller; principal-versus-agent/payment-services analysis; written provider and settlement-bank approval; country-specific tax, consumer, privacy, and data review; insurance; and a documented non-clinical service boundary.

## What Asa should launch

**Launch proposition:** “A trusted local representative helps you care for family in Tehran when you cannot be there.”

Start with two narrow services in a defined Tehran service area:

1. **Asa Shopping:** custom grocery and household shopping, with a budget, substitution rules, itemized reconciliation, delivery confirmation, and optional family update.
2. **Asa Medical Companion:** appointment coordination, practical non-clinical accompaniment, registration and waiting-room assistance, suitable transport coordination, and an authorized factual family update. No diagnosis, treatment, medication administration, clinical advice, or representation as a medical provider.

Keep **Asa Companion**, prepared meals, home support, and social activities as “Coming soon” until the team proves reliability, safeguarding, staffing, and unit economics.

## Best initial segment

The strongest early customer is an **adult child in the Iranian diaspora who has a parent in Tehran, has purchasing power, and experiences a specific time-sensitive task**: a difficult grocery run, a parent’s appointment, or uncertainty after the visit. The payer and recipient are different people, which makes trust evidence and reporting more valuable than generic delivery speed.

The diaspora is large enough to justify focused testing, but the public evidence is dated and not a direct market-size estimate. A Migration Policy Institute summary using 2019 UN estimates identified the United States, Canada, Germany, the UK, and Turkey as major destinations for Iranian immigrants.[1] The U.S. is the largest population opportunity but is also the most difficult legal/payment market for this model. Launch-market priority should therefore be determined by **payment clearance plus reachable communities**, not population alone.

## Launch geography

Use **one Tehran service zone**, not citywide coverage. Tehran is the most practical first test because it combines a large urban customer base, dense retail and healthcare infrastructure, and existing online/local alternatives. The evidence does not justify a claim that Tehran is the only viable city. A Tehran-first pilot should be narrowed further to 2–4 adjacent neighborhoods with predictable travel times and a vetted worker pool.

Iranian studies identify caregiver burden, weak institutional support, high time and money costs of medical visits, and a need for affordable, trained home-care support.[2] [3] A systematic review found unfavorable age-friendly conditions across Iranian cities, reinforcing the need for careful accompaniment and safeguarding rather than promising clinical care.[4]

## Revenue model

Use a **hybrid, transaction-led model**:

- Shopping: a transparent service/coordination fee plus delivery/travel fee. Grocery value is pass-through and must not be reported as service revenue.
- Medical Companion: a fixed visit package for a defined service window, plus a clearly stated additional-hour rate and transport pass-through.
- Recurring plans: introduce only after 20–30 paid orders demonstrate repeat demand. Start with prepaid bundles rather than automatic subscriptions.

Illustrative planning assumptions, not observed market prices:

- Shopping service revenue: **$18/order**; direct variable cost: **$9/order**.
- Medical Companion revenue: **$55/visit**; direct variable cost: **$35/visit**.
- Fixed monthly operating cost in the base case: **$4,500**.
- These assumptions are deliberately editable in the workbook and require local quotes and paid pilot validation.

## Financial conclusion

The model is attractive only if Asa protects service margin and avoids treating grocery pass-through as revenue. Under the base assumptions, the business reaches operating break-even at approximately **50 shopping orders plus 30 medical visits per month**, or a comparable contribution mix. The startup budget should be planned as a **$10,000–$20,000 range** before any payment/compliance retainer, insurance premium, or founder salary; the lower end assumes a founder-led pilot, no office, no inventory, no custom app, and a small vetted contractor pool.

The financial model is scenario-based, not a forecast supported by actual Asa sales. It should be updated weekly during validation.

## What must be true before the first paid order

- A named payment and settlement route has accepted the exact disclosed model in writing, or the sale is conducted through a legally reviewed alternative that does not create an unapproved Iran-bound payment flow.
- Asa has a defined contracting entity, owner/control record, tax position, and principal-versus-agent analysis.
- All counterparties are screened and the screening record is retained.
- Staff identity verification, references, safeguarding, incident escalation, insurance, and emergency contacts are in place.
- The service is genuinely non-clinical and the customer/recipient consent process is documented.
- Price, substitutions, refunds, cancellations, receipts, and family-update rules are clear.
- At least one small paid pilot can be delivered without relying on unsupported payment or settlement assumptions.

## Five priority founder decisions

1. **Choose the legally reviewable entity and payment route.** Evidence needed: provider/bank written approval and sanctions/payment-perimeter memo.
2. **Commit to a Tehran service zone.** Evidence needed: travel-time map, supplier/clinic interviews, and worker coverage.
3. **Choose the first wedge.** Test Shopping and Medical Companion separately; do not assume the combined offer will convert better.
4. **Approve the service boundary and trust system.** Evidence needed: insurance quote, safeguarding protocol, consent and reporting template.
5. **Run a paid, permissioned pilot.** Evidence needed: 20–30 completed orders with contribution margin, completion, complaint, refund, and repeat-purchase data.
